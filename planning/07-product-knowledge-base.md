## Evidence Classification

Every factual claim in this Knowledge Base should carry an evidence level. This prevents unsupported claims from accidentally becoming "facts" over time — especially as content cascades into AI systems, chatbots, and customer-facing channels.

### Canonical Claim Types

These are the five canonical evidence classifications. All factual claims across Foundation documents must use one of these labels.

| Type | Tag | Definition |
|------|-----|------------|
| 1 | **Engineering Verified** | Confirmed through product engineering or physical testing |
| 2 | **Manufacturing Specification** | From manufacturing partner documentation or process specs |
| 3 | **Customer Testimonial** | Sourced from customer reviews, Help Scout, or direct feedback |
| 4 | **Brand Positioning** | Strategic messaging that frames the product's market position |
| 5 | **Internal Guidance** | Operational direction for team use, not customer-facing |

**Implementation note:** Evidence tags will be added to individual claims incrementally. New claims entering this document must include an evidence level. Existing claims will be tagged during quarterly reviews. When in doubt, tag conservatively — it is better to mark a claim as "Brand Positioning" than to leave it untagged and have it treated as engineering-verified fact.

## 3. Fit & Sizing

### Canonical Answer

**Size Guide:**
- Performance Skins are designed to fit snugly like a second skin
- Color variations affect fit: Dark Grey, Hot Coral, and Blue run snugger than Light Grey

**Common fit scenarios:**
- **Too tight / toe pressure:** Size up. Dark Grey, Hot Coral, Blue run snugger.
- **Too loose / shifts during class:** Size down. If already in smallest size, a thin sock underneath works.
- **Narrow feet:** Try a thin toeless or individual-toe sock underneath. Dark Grey, Hot Coral, Blue naturally fit tighter.
- **Between sizes:** Go up half size. Use thin socks to fine-tune fit.

**Sizing philosophy:** Like finding the right shoe size — try on for fit, exchange if needed. Free shipping on size exchanges.

## 4. Wearing with Socks

### Canonical Answer

**Design intent:** Performance Skins are designed to be worn barefoot. That's the intended experience — maximum grip, barefoot control, toe splay.

**Three legitimate reasons to wear a thin sock underneath:**
1. **Perspiration in hot classes** — A thin sock acts as a moisture layer. Same grip performance, just adds sweat absorption.
2. **Hygiene preference** — Some practitioners prefer a barrier layer, especially in shared-equipment studios.
3. **Narrow feet / fit customization** — A thin sock adds volume for a snugger fit in the smallest sizes.

**Sock guidance:**
- Thin toeless or individual-toe socks work best (preserve toe splay)
- Skip thick or full-toe socks (reduces toe splay and grip contact)
- Hot yoga: thin full sock recommended for sweat absorption

**Studio compliance:** If a studio requires socks, wearing a thin sock underneath keeps you compliant while getting full Barreletics grip performance.

**Future note:** Barreletics socks are planned — designed specifically to complement Performance Skins. Build content architecture to accommodate this ecosystem.

### Surface Map
PDP (FAQ accordion) · FAQ page · Help Scout (can I wear socks? macro) · Tidio AI · Journal (care guide article)

### Abbreviated Version
Designed for barefoot wear. Thin sock underneath OK for sweat, hygiene, or fit adjustment. Skip thick socks — they reduce toe splay.

## 5. Durability & Longevity

### Canonical Answer

**Lifespan:** From months to years, from dozens of classes to over 1,000 — varies person to person based on frequency and intensity of use.

**Key durability facts:**
- Grip compound is injection-molded into the sole — it doesn't peel, flake, or wear off like silicone dots
- 360° grip surface maintains performance throughout the product life
- Material maintains shape — never stretches out like fabric socks
- Many customers report 3–4 years of regular use (6 days/week for 3+ years documented)

**Care affects longevity:** Pull from top of foot, not the straps. Proper cleaning extends life.

**What we DO NOT claim:** We do not guarantee a specific number of classes or months. We say "from months to years" because it genuinely varies.

## 6. Cleaning & Care

### Canonical Answer

**After class:**
- Warm soapy water, rinse, air dry
- Or wipe with equipment wipes post-class

**Key care points:**
- Non-porous material — doesn't absorb sweat or bacteria like fabric socks
- Antimicrobial properties — rinses clean, doesn't hold odor
- Air dry recommended (no machine dryer)
- No machine washing required (but not harmful if gentle cycle)

**Putting on / taking off:**
- Pull from the top of the foot, not the straps
- This protects the strap attachment points and extends product life

### Surface Map
PDP (accordion: Care) · FAQ page · Help Scout (care instructions macro) · Tidio AI · Product insert / packaging · Journal (care guide)

### Abbreviated Version
Rinse with warm soapy water after class. Air dry. Non-porous, antimicrobial — no bacteria buildup. Pull from top, not straps.

## 11. Barreletics vs Grip Socks

### Canonical Answer

This is the core category-creation comparison. Every mention should move the customer from "which grip sock?" to "why grip socks at all?"

| Feature | Traditional Grip Socks | Barreletics Performance Skins |
|---------|----------------------|-------------------------------|
| Grip | Silicone dots — inconsistent when wet, wear off | 360° full-contact surface — injection-molded grip won't peel or flake like silicone dots |
| Material | Fabric — absorbs sweat, harbors bacteria, stretches | Non-porous — rinse clean, antimicrobial, maintains shape |
| Cost | $14–$18/pair × 6–8 pairs/year = $112–$144/year | $74 one-time |
| Lifespan | 6–8 weeks per pair | 18+ months (many report 3–4 years) |
| Hygiene | Bacterial buildup, odor, machine wash required | Rinse and reuse, no bacterial absorption |
| Fit | Stretches out, bunches, requires frequent replacement | Second-skin fit, maintains shape |

**The Double Failure:** Grip socks fail in two ways simultaneously — silicone dots that wear off (grip failure) AND fabric that absorbs sweat and bacteria (material failure). Barreletics solves both.

**Sock Math:** One pair at $74 replaces 6–8 pairs at $14–$18 each. $112–$144/year on socks vs $74 once. Sock-cost figures are illustrative based on common DTC grip-sock pricing & lifespan ranges.
