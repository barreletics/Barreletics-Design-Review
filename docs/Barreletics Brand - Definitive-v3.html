How it all began

Our story

Patented Performance Skins for barre, Pilates, reformer, Megaformer, and studio fitness. Built because grip socks kept failing when grip mattered most.

Stefanie Miller, Founder

We didn’t improve the grip sock. We made it obsolete.

Filling a void

The only tool available kept interrupting the work.

At the time, grippy yoga socks were the only option. They slipped when grip mattered most, required constant adjusting, and never delivered the control they promised.

I found myself breaking focus to fix my socks instead of holding the position — sliding out of side planks, resetting my feet, losing stability where it counted.

Fabric holds moisture. I wanted a clean, connected practice — not a sock working against it.

If I was dedicating an hour, I wanted that hour to count. Secure. Grounded. Not distracted by what was happening beneath my feet.

And I knew I was not the only one.

The idea

Stuck in side plank. Sliding. Again.

It became clear this was not inconvenient. It was holding us back.

Why didn’t a better tool exist? What if there were flexible, non-slip, in-studio footwear with true all-over grip — something that could move from side plank to flat-back chair without slipping, shifting, or interrupting flow?

That same evening, I tore the silicone sleeve off a water bottle, cut it up, and made the first prototype.

Far from perfect. But it worked.

Two years

Trial and error, until the grip never left.

That on-the-fly prototype became the starting point. What followed was two years of testing, redesigning, and refining the details that mattered: pressure points, fit, flexibility, material, true all-over grip.

After countless iterations, Performance Skins were born — patented studio footwear, designed and manufactured in Michigan.

Built to feel secure and connected. Built to stay out of the way of the workout.

The Pilates sock era is over. A better way to grip is here.

The Pilates connection

The feet were always part of the system.

Joseph Pilates built Contrology on breath, concentration, control, precision, alignment, flow, and full-body awareness. The foot connects the body to the floor, the mat, the Reformer, the Cadillac, the Wunda Chair, the Megaformer — every surface beneath you.

Pilates depends on feedback. The foot has to sense, press, articulate, stabilize, and respond. A grip sock that slips or bunches moves your focus off form and onto your footing.

It is like holding a plank on a sliding towel. You can still do the move. Your body is compensating instead of engaging.

A practice built on control should not be compromised by what is under your feet.
