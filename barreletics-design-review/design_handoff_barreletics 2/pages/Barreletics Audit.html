Internal design review · barreletics.com

Same brand, nine sections, one ruleset.

The live theme has the ingredients of a strong brand — white, ink, and a punchy coral accent (the same #f93820 already used on the BR Media/Text Split CTA). But across templates, the same idea is built four different ways. This review documents what we found in the live theme code, locks the brand to a single token set, and proposes a global library of nine repeatable sections that every page composes from — reusing what’s already strong (BR Media/Text Split, the Coperni collab section, the variant grid) and adding what’s missing (a true full-bleed hero, a benefit grid, a journal).

01 — Executive summary

The brand is sound. The execution is fragmented.

The Barreletics value stack — safety, superior grip, replaces grip socks, easy to clean, hygiene, barefoot comfort, sweat performance, premium quality, non-toxic — is genuinely differentiated. The live theme already articulates each of those, often well, in isolated section blocks.

The problem isn’t messaging. It’s that the same value (e.g. “Stability”) is told with a 38px Roboto headline in one section, a 70px Josefin Sans headline in another, and an 90px uppercase headline in a third. The brand looks like three brands stacked on top of each other.

The fix isn’t a redesign. It’s consolidation:

  1. Lock the type ramp to one family at six fixed sizes.
  2. Lock the CTA system to three buttons — primary, secondary, tertiary.
  3. Retire the 14 one-off page templates in favor of nine composable sections assembled per template.
  4. Move all per-section font, color, padding settings into theme tokens — strip them from individual section schemas.

02 — The receipts

What the theme code actually shows.

Every claim below is pulled directly from config/settings_data.json and the live template JSON.

Finding 01 Typography drift

Two type families live in production where one is declared.

The theme’s base + header font is roboto_n4. But blocks inside homepage and PDP heroes override it with josefin_sans_n4, roboto_n3, and custom-font flags.

type_header_font_family: "roboto_n4"   ← theme default
type_base_font_family:   "roboto_n4"   ← theme default
↳ ss_hero_15_ifqFpA › heading_font:   "josefin_sans_n4"
↳ ss_hero_27_tHrrRH › text_font:      "josefin_sans_n4"
↳ ss_featured_collection_9 › title:   "roboto_n4" (custom: true)
↳ Per-block "_custom: true" overrides bypass the theme entirely.
Finding 02 CTA chaos

Buttons vary on every axis: size, weight, radius, padding, transform, color.

A single user can encounter 7+ distinct button styles between the home page hero, variant grid, and PDP. Mobile CTAs are particularly inconsistent.

button_size:        14, 18, 20, 21, 24      (6 sizes)
button_size_mobile: 14, 16, 18, 21, 24      (5 sizes)
button_radius:      0, 4, 8                 (3 radii)
button_padding_h:   20, 24, 28, 32, 34, 36, 78
button_padding_v:   8, 12, 14, 16
button_style:       outline, filled, non_outline_arrow, shadow
button_transform:   uppercase, normal       (mixed mid-page)
Finding 03 Hero overload

Six different hero section types are wired into the homepage alone.

Four of them are enabled simultaneously. Each ships with its own schema, its own settings, and renders headline / video / CTA differently. This is the root cause of the typography drift.

templates/index.json sections in use:
  br-multi-box-hero      (the strong one — keep, rename to br-collab grid)
  br-collab-hero         (Coperni — KEEP as is)
  br-media-text-split    (header section — KEEP as is)
  slideshow              ×4 instances        → DELETE
  hero-video             (legacy)            → DELETE
  ss-hero-14             ×2                  → DELETE
  ss-hero-27                                 → DELETE
→ Add ONE new section: br-hero (full-bleed, video/image, overlay text).
  Keep the 3 strong existing ones. Delete the 4 redundant ones.
Finding 04 Color drift

Five different “blacks” and three different “reds” on the live site.

The body-text token is #050505 but section blocks ship hard-coded colors. The sale palette is the worst offender — three different reds across badge, price, and CTA.

"black"  → #050505 (token) · #121212 · #1a1a1a · #2a2a2a · #333333
"red"    → #f93820 (BR media CTA — the live brand accent)
            · #f93822 (drift one digit off — drop)
            · #f75d44 (cart dot — secondary use only)
            · #990000 (sale tag — locked)
            · #cc0000 (sale price — drop)
→ Lock to: ink {#050505, #4a4a4a, #8a8a8a} ·
          accent #f93820 · sale #990000 only.
Finding 05 Template sprawl

14 one-off page templates and 8 one-off product templates.

Templates like page.about, page.compare-open-vs-closed, product.coperni, and product.in-studio-template-v-3 each carry their own copies of similar sections. Maintenance is exponential.

/templates breakdown:
  product.*  → 8 variants (39–72 KB each)
  page.*     → 14 variants (12–47 KB each)
  collection.* → 4 variants
→ Replace with: product.json + product.collab.json
            and: page.standard.json + page.long-form.json
Finding 06 Keep

The “Why it works” strip is the strongest brand element.

The 6-pillar uppercase strip (360° Grip · No Slip · Stay Secure · No Sock Fuss · Rinse & Reuse · No Latex) appears twice in the theme with slightly different copy. Standardize it, give it a permanent slot on every product & collection page.

Found in:
  templates/index.json     → br_variants_Jq8Hbh.custom_html
  templates/product.json   → br_variants_eezCUh.custom_html
  (Slightly different pillar text — unify.)
→ Promote to a standalone section: br-pillars
Finding 07 Watch

Voice slips between performance and lifestyle.

Same product, three voices: “Progress Built From The Ground Up!” · “Free yourself” · “Never Loses Shape”. Per the customer value stack, the conversion driver is safety + grip + replaces grip socks. The lifestyle voice is doing less work.

Recommended voice priorities (per customer value doc):
  1. Safety / stability        → primary hero copy
  2. Superior grip             → secondary hero / pillar strip
  3. Replaces grip socks       → category positioning
  4. Easy to clean / hygiene   → PDP benefit grid
  5. Barefoot feel             → product story
Finding 08 Dead settings

The “cream + plum” header/footer in settings_data.json is dead code.

The settings file declares color_header: #fff6f0 and color_header_text: #4f1c3a, but css-variables.liquid maps --colorNav to --colorBody (white) and --colorNavText to --colorBodyText (ink). The header and footer have always rendered as white + ink on the live site. Drop the cream/plum settings entirely — they confuse future merchants and waste editor surface area.

/* in snippets/css-variables.liquid */
--colorNav:     {{ settings.color_body_bg | default: "#fff" }};
--colorNavText: {{ settings.color_body_text | default: "#000" }};

/* settings_data.json declares but NEVER applies: */
"color_header":        "#fff6f0"   ← dead
"color_header_text":   "#4f1c3a"   ← dead
"color_footer":        "#fff6f0"   ← dead
"color_footer_text":   "#4f1c3a"   ← dead
"color_borders":       "#4f1c3a"   ← dead

→ Strip from settings_schema.json so they stop showing up
  in the merchant theme editor.

05 — Call-to-action system (locked)

Three buttons. That’s the whole CTA library.

Primary on white, secondary on white, tertiary as text link with underline. Two adaptations: on-image (border becomes white) and invert (for dark sections). No new variants.

Primary
Shop Performance Skins

Solid ink. Used once per section maximum. The conversion CTA.

.br-btn--primary
Secondary
See the collaboration

Outline. Paired with primary when two equal actions exist (rare).

.br-btn--secondary
Tertiary
Read the story

Inline text link with underline. Replaces every “learn more” chevron.

.br-btn--tertiary
On dark / on image
Shop now See the collaboration

What this kills: the button_style: "non_outline_arrow" chevron CTAs in ss-hero-14, the orange #f93822 button on the PDP, the 78px-horizontal-padding mobile button in br-multi-box-hero, and the four other one-off styles in section blocks.

06 — The eight sections

Every page composes from this set.

Home, PDP, collection, blog, and content pages all use only these eight. Each has a fixed schema and pulls every visual property from theme tokens — no per-instance font, color, or padding overrides.

01

Hero

Headline + eyebrow + 1 CTA. Single, split, or multi-box layout. Replaces 6 hero types.
02

Pillar strip

The 6-pillar “Why it works” strip. Sticky brand reinforcement on every product & collection.
03

Variant grid

Filtered product/color grid. Closed Sole · Open Sole · Outdoor · One-offs. Already strong.
04

Story block

Two-column image + text. For brand story, fabric story, founder, collaborator. Replaces text-and-image.
05

Testimonial

Single quote with attribution. Replaces the testimonial section + repeated quote callouts on PDP.
06

Benefit grid

2×3 or 3×2 benefit grid. Anchor on PDP; also for “non-toxic / no latex / quick dry” explainers.
07

Product rail

Horizontal carousel of related/featured product. Replaces ss-featured-collection-9.
08

Editorial / journal

Blog index, article card grid, and long-form article body. One layout, three contexts.

Header, footer, announcement bar, and PDP main are constants — they wrap every page and aren’t counted here. Cart, search, and account pages use platform-default chrome.

07 — Section mocks

Each section, two or three layout variations.

Click the tabs to flip between variations. Every variation uses the locked tokens — different layout, same type ramp, same buttons. Imagery is placeholder; real product/lifestyle photography drops in.
