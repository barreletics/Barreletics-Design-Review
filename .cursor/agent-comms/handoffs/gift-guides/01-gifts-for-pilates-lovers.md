## 3. Body HTML (paste as is)

```html
<h2 class="article-dek">Useful gifts for the person who never misses a reformer class.</h2>
<p class="article-lead">Pilates people tend to be particular about their gear. This guide skips the novelty items and sticks to gifts that get used: things for the studio, things for home, and one pick of our own. Barreletics makes grip shoes, so we are not neutral on that one, and we say so where it comes up.</p>
<h2>Start with three questions</h2>
<p>Do they go to a studio or practice at home? Do you know their shoe size? What do you want to spend? Those three answers cut the list down fast. If all you know is that they love Pilates, a class pack at their regular studio is the safest bet. If you know what they do every week, buy something for that routine.</p>
<p>It also helps to remember what they complain about. A person who is always hunting for a clean towel, or always short of something to wear on their feet, is telling you what to buy.</p>
<h2>Gifts for studio regulars</h2>
<h3>1. A class pack or gift card from their own studio</h3>
<p>Most studios sell gift cards or class packs, and many sell them online. Pick the studio they already go to, not the one you like, because they will use it without a second thought. Prices vary by studio and city, so check the studio's site or ask at the front desk.</p>
<h3>2. A private session with their instructor</h3>
<p>A one on one session lets them work on whatever they find hard, with someone who already knows their body. Ask the instructor about availability and price before you buy, and tell them it is a gift so they can plan around it.</p>
<h3>3. Barreletics Performance Skin, $74 for most colors</h3>
<p>This one is ours, so weigh it accordingly. Barreletics Performance Skin is a grip shoe made for Pilates, barre and reformer classes. Founder Stefanie Miller designed it after she could not get a grip during barre practice, and she built the grip into the sole instead of adding dots. There is no latex and no silicone.</p>
<p>There are two styles. <a href="/products/best-reformer-pilates-legree-workout-shoes">Closed Sole</a> covers the whole bottom of the foot, heel included. <a href="/products/studio-performance-skin-footwear">Open Sole</a> has an open heel and a vent at the mid foot for a more barefoot feel. Toes stay free in both, and the product pages say the grip and stability are the same, so the choice comes down to how it feels. Most colors are $74, and a few limited colors are $78.</p>
<p>Some people are happy in grip socks, and socks cost less. Others find that socks stretch, bunch or lose their grip after a lot of washes. If your person is in that second group, this is a gift they will notice. We wrote more on that in <a href="/blogs/news/why-grip-socks-fail-barre-pilates-what-to-wear-instead">why grip socks fall short</a>. Check whether their studio has rules about footwear first, since some ask for socks or bare feet.</p>
<h3>4. A water bottle and a quick dry towel</h3>
<p>These are not exciting, and that is the point. Reformer and mat classes are warm work, and nobody ever has too many towels. Pick a bottle that fits in a cup holder and a bag side pocket, and a towel that folds small. Check the price and size on each before you buy, because both vary a lot.</p>
<h3>5. A bag that holds the whole kit</h3>
<p>A roomy tote or a small duffel that fits shoes, a towel, a bottle and a change of clothes saves a lot of fumbling before class. Look for a bag with one separate pocket for damp items.</p>
<h2>Gifts for home practice</h2>
<h3>6. A Pilates ring, small ball or light bands</h3>
<p>These props are small, easy to store and cheap enough to buy as a set. They suit someone who does a few minutes at home between studio visits. If they already follow a video series or an app, you can check what props it uses and buy to match.</p>
<h3>7. A massage ball for tired feet and shoulders</h3>
<p>A firm little ball is the sort of gift people use for years and never think to buy for themselves. It fits in a stocking and in a gym bag. Hard rolling on a sore spot is not for everyone, so if they have an injury or pain, skip this one and ask them what their physical therapist or instructor suggests.</p>
<h3>A note on mats</h3>
<p>Do not buy a main mat unless they have told you exactly which one they want. People who practice often have strong opinions about thickness and surface, and a mat is hard to swap once it has been used.</p>
<h2>How to buy something that needs a size</h2>
<p>Footwear and clothing are the riskiest gifts, so make it easy on yourself. Find out their size without asking directly if you want it to be a surprise: check the shoes by their door, or ask a friend or partner. Then use the <a href="/pages/performance-skins-size-chart">Barreletics size chart</a> instead of guessing.</p>
<p>Barreletics offers 30 day returns, as long as the pair is clean, like new and only tried on indoors. Size exchanges are free to ship once the return is inspected, and they need to start within 30 days of delivery. Orders over $150 ship free, and smaller orders have a flat shipping rate. If you want to see everything before you decide, the full <a href="/collections/barre-pilates-yoga-shoe-sock-footwear">grip shoe collection</a> is in one place.</p>
<h2>A simple way to choose</h2>
<p>If you know their studio, buy the class pack. If you know their size, buy the grip shoes. If you know neither, buy a towel and a bottle, add a handwritten note, and let the gift be small and useful. Looking for something for a friend who does barre or yoga instead? Try our <a href="/blogs/news/gifts-for-barre-and-yoga-people">gifts for barre and yoga people</a>, or the <a href="/blogs/news/stocking-stuffers-and-secret-santa-gifts-under-50">stocking stuffers and Secret Santa ideas under $50</a>.</p>
<h2>Questions people ask about Pilates gifts</h2>
<h3>What is a good gift for someone who does Pilates every week?</h3>
<p>A class pack or gift card for their own studio is the safest choice. After that, pick something they use up or wear out, such as a towel, a bottle or a pair of grip shoes.</p>
<h3>Are grip socks or grip shoes better for Pilates?</h3>
<p>It depends on the person. Socks cost less and many people are happy in them. Some find socks stretch, bunch or lose their grip over time, which is why Barreletics makes grip shoes. Check the studio's footwear rules before you decide.</p>
<h3>How much do Barreletics Performance Skins cost?</h3>
<p>At the time of writing, most colors are $74 and a few limited colors are $78. Both Open Sole and Closed Sole are priced the same for most colors.</p>
<h3>What if I pick the wrong size?</h3>
<p>You can exchange within 30 days of delivery. The pair needs to be clean, like new and only tried on indoors. Size exchanges ship free once the return is inspected. Start with the size chart to get it right the first time.</p>
<h3>Can they wear Performance Skins for yoga or barre too?</h3>
<p>Yes. The product pages list reformer, mat Pilates, barre and yoga on or off the mat as uses.</p>
```
